🌋 Volcano Research Project

In groups of 2–3, you will become the class experts on a real volcano. You’ll research the science, build a model, teach the class, and create a worksheet.

How does your volcano’s geology shape human life in its region/country?

    Guided Research

    What you’re turning in

    The guided research packet replaces a traditional essay. Use short phrases and bullets. Be ready to explain every answer.

    Open Guided Research (PDF) Sources (USGS / GVP)

    Tip: Use Smithsonian Global Volcanism Program for eruption history and volcano type.

    Minimum research expectations

    • Plate setting → magma type → eruption style (explain why)
    • One major eruption case study (what happened + hazards + impacts)
    • Hazards today + how scientists monitor the volcano
    • Culture section focuses on the region/country (food, religion/beliefs, economy, adaptation)

    Model Requirements

    Required labels

    • Magma chamber
    • Conduit (pipe)
    • Main vent
    • Crater (or caldera, if appropriate)

    What makes it “Exceeds”

    • Model shape/type matches your assigned volcano
    • Includes layers/flows/hazards appropriate to the volcano
    • Neat, sturdy, and easy to interpret from 5 feet away

    Eruption is optional

    If you do an eruption demo, it must be safe and quick. You will not earn points for mess. You earn points for accurate structure and labeling.

    Presentation

    Time + participation

    • 5–7 minutes total
    • Every member must speak
    • Don’t read slides (use them as visuals)

    Must include

    • Map (where it is)
    • Volcano type + magma type
    • Plate setting explanation
    • Hazards (at least 3) + one case study eruption
    • Culture/region connection
    • Sources listed (last slide)

    Fast way to be memorable

    Teach one “cause → effect” chain clearly: plate setting → magma → viscosity/gas → eruption style → hazards → human impact.

    Class Worksheet

    Worksheet requirements

    • 5–7 questions (quality over quantity)
    • At least 1 vocabulary question
    • At least 1 diagram or map question
    • At least 1 hazard scenario question (“What would you do if…?”)
    • Answer key required

    Approval rule

    Your worksheet must be approved by the teacher before presentation day. Unapproved worksheets may be returned for revision.

    Detailed Rubric Table

    Category Points Exceeds / Meets / Developing / Beginning
    A. Guided Research 30
    Exceeds (30): All sections complete, accurate, and explained with “why” (plate setting → magma → eruption style). Culture focuses on the region/country and connects to environment/economy/adaptation.
    Meets (25): Most sections complete and mostly accurate; correct volcano type + plate setting; hazards listed with basic explanation; culture included with basic regional details.
    Developing (18): Several sections incomplete/vague OR includes scientific errors; hazards/monitoring generic; culture minimal or not regional.
    Beginning (8): Major missing sections, major inaccuracies, or copied info with little understanding.
    B. Model 25
    Exceeds (25): Model matches assigned volcano type/shape; labeled clearly; includes correct layers/flows/hazards; neat and sturdy.
    Meets (21): Correct overall type; most required labels; mostly accurate features; stable construction.
    Developing (15): Missing several labels; generic volcano; messy or hard to interpret.
    Beginning (7): Missing model OR missing most required features/labels.
    C. Presentation 25
    Exceeds (25): Teaches clearly without reading; strong visuals (map/diagram); all members speak; explains cause/effect; answers questions confidently; on time.
    Meets (21): Mostly clear; required visuals present; most members speak; minor pacing/reading issues.
    Developing (15): Frequent reading or unclear explanation; missing required element; uneven participation.
    Beginning (7): Off-topic, too short/long, missing major elements, or little preparation.
    D. Worksheet + Key 20
    Exceeds (20): 5–7 strong questions aligned to presentation; includes vocab + map/diagram + hazard scenario; answer key complete; formatted clearly for classmates.
    Meets (17): Meets question requirements; key mostly complete; minor clarity/alignment issues.
    Developing (12): Too short/too easy/not aligned; missing a required question type; key incomplete.
    Beginning (6): Worksheet missing/unusable OR no answer key.
    Important Notes —
    • Sources: minimum of 2 credible sources listed.
    • Individual adjustment: teacher may apply up to -50% for lack of contribution.
    • Time: presentations must fit the 5–7 minute window.
