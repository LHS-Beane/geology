Geologic Timeline Project

Create a 10-foot, to-scale timeline of Earth’s 4.6 billion year history, then build a separate zoom-in of the last 20,000 years of human history. Your goal is to show just how tiny human history is compared to geologic time.

Project Overview

You and your partner will create two connected timeline products:

  1. A 10-foot geologic timeline showing major events in Earth’s history to scale.
  2. A human history zoom-in showing the last 20,000 years with major world, science, technology, and cultural events.
Big Idea: The point of this project is not just to place dates. It is to help you understand scale. Most of Earth’s history happened long before humans appeared.
Work in pairs Balanced: conceptual + scale Neat and readable Include visuals

Materials

  • 10-foot paper strip or butcher paper section
  • Ruler or tape measure
  • Pencils and erasers
  • Markers or colored pencils
  • Reference sheet / class notes
  • Separate paper section for human history zoom

Tip: Lightly pencil all measurements first. Add color and visuals only after your event placement is checked.

Project Directions

  1. Set up the main timeline.
    Measure the full 10 feet. Label the far left as Earth forming and the far right as present day.
  2. Lightly mark the major geologic events.
    Use the scale provided. Check your placements before decorating.
  3. Add labels and short descriptions.
    Each required event should be clearly named and easy to read.
  4. Add visuals.
    Use icons, sketches, color coding, or small illustrations that actually help explain the timeline.
  5. Create the human history zoom.
    Use the second scale to show the last 20,000 years in more detail.
  6. Answer the reflection questions.
    These should show that you understand why scale matters.

Reflection Questions

  1. Why is human history so small compared to geologic time?
  2. What surprised you most while building your timeline?
  3. Why do scientists divide Earth’s history into sections?

Partner Roles

  • Partner A: Scale Manager — handles measurements, placement, and checking scale
  • Partner B: Design Lead — handles visuals, labels, and layout

Both partners are responsible for understanding the project and completing the reflection questions.
